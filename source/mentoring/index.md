AI mentoring with Gleb Kalinin

I like helping people make something real out of an idea that feels slightly beyond them. You might have a project you keep postponing, a way of working you can imagine but haven’t figured out how to build, or something you would love to try if you knew where to begin.

AI gives us a lot to work with here. Learning to use it also involves attention, curiosity, frustration, habits, and the willingness to be a beginner, so all of those are part of our work together.

My background spans technology, product work, psychology, coaching, and creative practice. I draw on these to help you design systems that fit how you work, and to understand what makes it difficult to put them into practice.

## We follow your interest

“I should learn AI” is a difficult place to start. I’d rather begin with something you are curious about, something you would like to make, or a task that keeps taking more effort than it deserves. There may also be something you’ve assumed you couldn’t do yourself that is worth another look.

We look for a project you have a reason to return to, whether that’s writing, working with your notes, exploring a research question, building a prototype, or rethinking a recurring task. It can be small or ambitious, as long as it gives us a place to begin.

## What you might bring

Here are a few examples of requests from recent AI consultations and earlier coaching around personal knowledge and ways of working:

- **From scattered chats to a repeatable workflow.** You already use AI, just not systematically—copy-pasting between chats and documents, with everything depending on what you remember to do manually. Together we can take one recurring task and turn it into a clear, repeatable workflow with the right context attached, so each run leaves you with an organised next step to build on.
- **Meeting notes and decisions.** Your notes and incoming information are scattered, and you want a reliable way to capture them, pull up past decisions before a call, and sketch an agenda—for example, figuring out how Teams transcripts could flow into a local knowledge base.
- **Research and creative projects.** You have articles, exhibition notes, references, and half-formed ideas that need to connect around one project, so you can see its layers clearly and explain the work to others.
- **Ideas, writing, and visual material.** You collect professional thoughts, drafts, and photographs, and want a repeatable routine for choosing the next step and sharing your work publicly.
- **Projects, people, and follow-ups.** Business ideas, contacts, reading, and commitments live in different places, and you want to see who is doing what and what needs attention without forcing everyone into a single task manager.

## We do some of the work together

During a session, I like to work on a piece of your actual task. We open your material, try an approach, look at what happens, and work out the next step. Starting together often makes continuing on your own much easier.

Depending on what you need, I might explain or demonstrate something, ask questions, or suggest that we reconsider the goal. I pay attention to how you describe the problem, because the way we formulate it can change what becomes possible. Along the way, we develop ways of thinking and making decisions that you can use again.

Between meetings, you try things in your own work, and when we meet again we look at what happened. If an approach failed, felt awkward, or never got used, that gives us something to investigate and adjust.

## I am on your side

You can arrive confused, frustrated, or having done none of what you planned, and we can be honest about that without turning it into a judgement of you. I work from partnership, mutual respect, and the belief that people can change without being punished into it.

I will also ask you to think, make choices, and stay with a difficult problem when it deserves the effort. Some things are complex, and part of our work is learning to stay with that complexity long enough to find a manageable next action.

Your circumstances matter too: the time and energy you have, the people you work with, and the environment you are trying to change. We take those into account when deciding what is realistic and what might need to change around you.

## What changes between meetings matters

A conversation can be interesting and leave your daily life exactly as it was. I want us to notice whether you are actually using what we explore: returning to the project, doing something that used to stop you, or getting better at deciding what to ask, what to check, and what to do next.

Small, repeated actions can give you evidence that you can trust yourself to follow through. Over time, that makes bigger changes easier to approach and gives you more confidence to continue independently.

## Bring something you care about

You don’t need a technical background or a neatly formulated request, but you do need some willingness to experiment and make room for practice between meetings.

Write to me about what you are trying to do, what interests you, and where you are getting stuck. We can start there.
